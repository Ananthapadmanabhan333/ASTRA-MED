## Limitations

- All data is simulated by the ASTRA-MED simulator. Results show how well each method recovers the simulator's injected events and dynamics; they say nothing about accuracy on real astronauts.
- The simulator's physiology, the injected events and the evaluation labels share one design, which makes the task easier than real monitoring, where events are unlabelled, heterogeneous and rare.
- Planned activity equals actual activity in the simulator; real crew timelines deviate from plan.
- Detection thresholds are calibrated on labelled validation data to a 1% window false-alarm rate. A real deployment would have no labels and would need clinician-reviewed calibration.
- Prediction intervals are split-conformal; their coverage guarantee assumes exchangeability, which time series only approximate. Test coverage is measured and reported, not assumed.
- No model here has been validated on astronaut or patient populations. Outputs are experimental decision support and must never be used as a diagnosis.
